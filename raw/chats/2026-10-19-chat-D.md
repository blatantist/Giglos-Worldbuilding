The closest named fit is **eudaimonism** (flourishing ethics). The modern version most like what you describe is the **capabilities approach** (Sen, Nussbaum): the goal is a healthy baseline where an individual or group can actually function and succeed, rather than maximizing pleasure. For your Giglians specifically, **Spinoza's Ethics** may be the best source:

- **Joy** is defined as a transition to greater power to act and exist, not as pleasure.
- **Sadness** is defined purely relationally, as a transition to lesser power. There's no independent negative emotion, only a decrease in joy.
- Nearly every other emotion is derived from these two plus desire, so the whole affect system hangs off joy.

That maps well onto your "negative described in relation to the positive" idea. A related concept is the **privation theory** (Augustine), where evil is just the absence of good, if you want a more religious or doctrinal flavor for their courts.

For the laughter-as-speech idea, good speech as controlled laughter fits naturally with joy as expansion of capacity. Bad speech or bad states could be framed as "laughter withheld" or "laughter that failed to land," so the grammar itself encodes the privation view.
