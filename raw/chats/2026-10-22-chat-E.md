Your trick already shows the central consequence: with no free tongue tip, "place of articulation" stops being tip versus body and becomes where along the roof the pad closes the tube. Here is what that predicts.

- **Nasals vanish.** No nose means no /m n ŋ/ and no nasal vowels. What a human hears as "m" would be a plain voiced labial stop. It also means the oral/nasal distinction is absent, so any nasal-like color would have to come from pharyngeal constriction.
- **Coronals mostly collapse.** /t d s z l r/ all lean on a mobile tip. You would keep labial sounds (beak/lip), palatal and velar sounds made by raising the pad (your dental-fricative workaround is basically a palatal fricative), pharyngeal sounds, and glottal sounds. That is a small, human-approximable inventory, which matches your goal.
- **Vowels run on tube shape, not tongue position.** With the tongue fixed, F1 comes from jaw/pad opening, and F2 comes from lip rounding and pharynx width instead of front/back tongue placement. The vowel space shrinks, so contrasts likely shift to length, pitch, and phonation type (breathy, creaky). That fits your lore that pitch and rhythm carry a lot of meaning.
- **Variable tube length is a global formant knob.** If larynx height or neck length changes the tube, formant shift becomes prosody, so a speaker could shade a whole utterance up or down the way humans use pitch. That is a good hook for emotional register in a species with only positive valence.
- **Giggling falls out naturally.** A giggle is rhythmic glottal pulsing, so the baseline syllable could be a pulse (/h/ or glottal gating) carrying a vowel, with consonants as modulations of that airstream. It also suggests flutter or trill sounds from the pad against the palate.

If the beak center is stiff and hard, you also get something humans lack: percussive beak taps and clacks that can work like click consonants, with no teeth needed. Whether that happens depends on how hard the beak is and whether they chew with it.

Can the pad raise at several points independently (front, middle, back), or does it only move as one piece? That decides whether you get a real palatal versus velar contrast or just one closure place plus the labials and pharyngeals.
