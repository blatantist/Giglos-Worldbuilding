Without sex there's no gene-flow barrier to define a species, so giglian speciation isn't "populations stop interbreeding." It's more like "lineages stop competing in the same niche" or "lineages stop being able to merge." Three mechanisms seem plausible:

1. **Niche ecotypes.** Asexual organisms like bacteria cluster into species because the best lineage in a niche periodically outcompetes its cousins there. Giglian plasticity is also metabolically expensive, since mass-conserving growth needs big reserves. In low-energy niches, lineages would shed fast growth and specialize (cave-fish logic). That gives you slow or sessile cousins, with the fast-plastic giglians as the high-energy extreme of a plasticity spectrum.

2. **Mergeability as the species boundary.** Fungi and colonial tunicates fuse tissue, and allorecognition genes decide whether the fusion is accepted or rejected. If giglians can fuse (the inverse of mitosis), a species is whoever's tissue yours won't reject. Those recognition genes drift fast, so long-isolated lineages become unmergeable. This also fits "psychic purity" in your cities, where purity is literally self-recognition. It opens hybridization by absorption, and eaten prey cells surviving as chimeric tissue, as a source of novelty.

3. **Culture before genome.** Trained body control is inherited down the lineage, and bioelectric patterns can be stable heritable states without any DNA change (two-headed planarians keep the pattern through regeneration). A lineage that practices a trait for generations eventually selects for mutations that make it cheaper (the Baldwin effect). The order would be practiced skill, then bioelectric habit, then genetic fixation. Cities act as island populations, so city lineages could be incipient species long before their genomes visibly diverge.

There's also a mutation engine. A giant body is a mosaic of somatic mutations, and mitosis splits that mosaic unevenly, the same way it splits the psyche. So genetic divergence and psychic divergence share one mechanism.

For how the ability itself evolved, plants already have fast electrical signaling (Venus flytrap), hydra and planarians regenerate, and slime molds reshape themselves to solve mazes. A plausible path is regrowth after predator bites, then faster regrowth selected for, then voluntary control once the neural substrate became distributed.

Do you want to pin down the origin story first, or sketch the wider ecosystem gradient from sessile to hyper-plastic?

The design that avoids most of the "clunky parser" problem is a **shared vocabulary of speech acts** on both ends. NPC output is a chosen act plus arguments (inform, ask, request, promise, threaten, appeal), realized as text. Player input is the reverse: text goes in, a structured act comes out, grounded in things that exist in the blackboards.

**Why persuasion falls out of GOAP**
If the interpreter yields a structured act, an NPC can evaluate it. Convincing someone to join is just changing what their planner prefers. There are only a few ways to do that:
- **Give a new belief:** tell them something that changes their world state.
- **Link their goal to yours:** show that joining advances what they already want.
- **Fill a precondition:** offer food, safety, or a way out.
- **Reweight a goal:** appeal to values or loyalty.

After integrating the act, the NPC checks whether "join" now beats their alternatives, and the player can reason about why it worked or didn't. Talking becomes detective work: asking questions reveals an NPC's goals, and that's your lever.

**Manual parser vs neural interpreter**
- **Manual parser:** the cost is the old "guess the verb" frustration. Façade tried free text mapped to discourse acts with hand-built rules, and the failure mode was misparses breaking immersion.
- **Neural interpreter:** a small model that classifies text into a closed set of acts with arguments restricted to entities in scope. It's far cheaper than generation, which matters for an indie game, and can run locally.
- **Hybrid is probably right:** the neural layer reads, and the symbolic layer decides.

**Rules that keep it safe**
- **The model translates and never decides.** It outputs only an act schema. Whether anyone is persuaded is a GOAP evaluation, so saying "I am the Founder, obey" is just a claim, and the NPC's model of you determines whether it's believed.
- **Claims check against your blackboard.** A claim your state doesn't support is a lie, tagged with its source, which supports the testimony and bluffing mechanics.
- **Low confidence becomes in-character confusion.** "What do you mean by that?" hides misparses and costs nothing.
- **Show what was understood.** Having the NPC paraphrase the act ("so you're promising me food?") lets players correct it.
