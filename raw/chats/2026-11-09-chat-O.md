A brown dwarf fits Giglos well. A red dwarf works too, but it would rewrite the climate you've already set up. Mass is what drives everything here: both are about Jupiter's size, but brown dwarfs weigh about 13–75 Jupiters and the smallest red dwarfs about 80+.

**What changes either way:**
- **Everything moves farther out.** To keep the 11-day orbit, distances grow with the cube root of the mass. A 30-Jupiter brown dwarf puts Giglos at about 4.3M km instead of about 1.4M. An 80-Jupiter red dwarf puts it at about 6M. The inner moons and the 1 : 2 : 4 rhythm scale out the same way, and stability is fine.
- **The ring survives.** A heavier, denser host pushes the ring zone out too, to roughly 500k km for 30 Jupiters. That's still well inside the inner moons, so the shepherd setup still works.
- **Eclipses get rarer.** The host is the same size but Giglos is farther away, so the shadow covers a smaller slice of the orbit. That suits what you want.
- **Planets between it and the main star:** the catch. A companion at about 2.7× Earth's distance makes a fairly close binary. Planets around the main star would only stay stable within roughly a quarter to a third of that, about 0.7–0.9× Earth's distance. A few hot inner planets fit there. More room means a wider binary, which also lengthens the 4-year year.

**Brown dwarf (about 20–40 Jupiters): the easy fit**
- It glows a dim deep red or magenta, mostly in infrared. At Giglos it might give around 1% of Earth's sunlight.
- That's enough to see by, and enough for some warmth. It doesn't overpower the main sun.
- The near side's night gets a permanent red glow overhead, while the far side gets true dark. That fits the existing near-side/far-side climate split in the wiki.
- It fades slowly over billions of years. That's a possible deep-time or religious hook.

**Red dwarf (about 80+ Jupiters): dramatic, but heavy**
- At about 6M km it would give Giglos roughly 30% of Earth's sunlight. That's about as much as the main star gives (about a third).
- Because Giglos is tidally locked, the near side would never get a real night. The 11-day cycle would only hold on the far side, and you'd have two very different hemispheres.
- Red dwarfs flare, with ultraviolet and X-ray bursts that strip atmospheres. The thick air and clouds help, but it's a real threat.
- You'd likely need to dim the main star, or move the planet's orbit, to avoid overheating. That reopens Chat J's ~4-year orbit and brighter sun.

**My pick:** a mid-mass brown dwarf. You get the "the planet glows" effect and the planets-between-stars option, while the 11-day cycle, eclipses, ring and climate stay mostly as they are. All of this is my suggestion ([C]) unless you adopt it.
