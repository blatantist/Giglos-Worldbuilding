Mass conservation actually makes the deformation mechanics more interesting, not less, because it forces you to decide what's trading off against what.

**Stretching**: if mass is fixed and you elongate, cross-sectional area has to shrink, same as pulling taffy. That gives you a natural weakness curve for free, a giglian reaching for a high shelf just thins out proportionally, but past some point the cartilage-analog structural elements can't span that thin a cross-section without losing cohesion, so there's a hard limit on reach that's about structural integrity, not some arbitrary "stretch cap."

**Inflating** is the trickier one, since pure mass conservation says you can't just get bigger. The clean fix is that it's not actually inflation, it's aeration, a trained giglian can whip their own cytoplasm into a foam (like meringue) to increase volume while keeping mass constant, trading density for size. That's mechanically consistent and also gives you a nice tell: an inflated giglian would visibly look frothy/bubbled rather than smoothly bloated, since they're incorporating gas into the tissue rather than "creating" mass.

Where it gets fun is the gum-gum fist specifically, because aerating a fist to make it bigger should make the punch weaker per unit area, not stronger (lower density = less momentum per volume). A skilled giglian's haymaker would be about redirecting momentum through a rapid taper, not bulk. That also nicely means raw size stops mattering much past a threshold and technique/control does, which is a good excuse to have skill hierarchies or martial traditions that aren't just "biggest giglian wins."
