If mitosis is a mechanic, every recruit, ally, or sacrifice can come with the question of how you divide yourself. - **Recombine the cast-offs**: the revolution literally merges the discarded into something bigger than the regime's leader

Those two extremes line up with the two sides of your plot, which is what makes them work. The all-self-mitose route is how the founder built the city in the first place: near-identical psyches, low greed, high cooperation, zero friction. Played to the end, it turns the protagonist into the regime. The all-recruit route is the opposite: a coalition of divergent, generationally distant giglians who bring skills you can't get any other way, but who argue, defect, and have their own agendas. So the neutral route is just the political question "how much do you trust people who aren't you?"

The risk is that one extreme becomes strictly optimal. Self-copies are loyal and predictable, which is usually the stronger pick in an RPG, so it needs real costs. Your lore already supplies them:

- **Splitting dilutes you.** Mitosis divides mass and psyche unevenly, so each copy could carry away some of your skills, dialogue options, or personality traits. A pure self-army leaves the protagonist hollow, with fewer conversation choices and a narrower build, surrounded by specialists who each embody a piece of you.
- **Copies share your blind spots.** An army of you agrees with you, which means it can't tell you you're wrong. That could be literal: certain events only flag or resolve when someone disagrees with you.
- **Recruits cost friction, not mass.** Their price is loyalty management and the chance they walk, but they carry skill lineages you never had.

The neutral route gets interesting if the two aren't just a slider. Self-copies could be your reliable specialists for fixed jobs, while recruits handle the things that need outside perspective or legitimacy. Other giglians would also notice a protagonist who's building an army of themselves, which lets the world reactively compare you to the founder.

Should the food and mass cost of splitting be a hard gate on the self-mitose route, or do you want the dilution alone to be the brake?

Quick version of how Disco Elysium handles it, mechanics only:

- **Structure:** four attributes with six skills each. You spread a small point pool across the attributes at the start, each skill begins at its attribute's value, and an attribute also limits how high its skills can climb.
- **Skills are voices.** They interrupt dialogue with their own opinions. Some lines only appear if a skill is high enough (passive checks), and others are dice rolls against a difficulty (active checks).
- **Failure has two flavors.** Some checks can be retried once you've improved the skill, and some are one shot. Those one-shot ones are where permanent lock-in comes from.
- **The Thought Cabinet:** ideas you slot in and spend time internalizing, with bonuses and penalties. Your choices push you toward them.

Your mitosis system fits that frame well. Some observations:

**The math produces your lore for free.** With the 20% bonus, n splits give 2^n bodies at 0.6^n of the original points each, so total power grows by 1.2^n while each individual shrinks. That is exactly the city-seeding picture: plenty of aggregate capability, nobody individually formidable. Distance down the chain becomes literal dilution.

**The unbalanced perk is greedy mitosis.** Your culture treats it as the central moral sin, so unlocking it should be an in-world act with NPCs reacting, not a neutral perk.

**Unspent points are a hidden respec.** Since they can be freely distributed at a split, hoarding them buys flexibility at the cost of being weaker until then. That's a good tension, but decide on it deliberately.

**The biggest risk is routing around your own lock-in.** If the player controls both halves in conversation, any closed option just gets handled by the other half. Either one half is the "you" the dialogue runs through, or swapping speakers needs a cost (the other half must be present, trusted, or alive). Skills you gave away could also show up as voices that remember how but can't act, which fits the shared memories in your lore.

Does the player stay as one half after a split, or switch between them?

The balance question is really concentration versus coverage, not total points. A skill check only ever uses one actor's points, so 100 in one body beats 60 + 60 for any gated check, and the total only matters for breadth. A few consequences:

- **Non-mitose probably doesn't need extra points.** Its edge is depth with no split cost, and that's already strong if content has steep thresholds. The clean split's 20% and up is its payment for breadth: two bodies in two places, two skill sets, and redundancy if one half dies. Whether non-mitosis is overpowered depends on how many high-threshold checks your content has compared to broad ones, so tune it there.
- **Greedy mitosis is already the best path per body.** Max-uneven keeps 100% concentrated in one body, which is exactly what the leader did. Nerfing its total feels arbitrary, so I'd price it in the world instead. Each greedy split leaves a persistent discarded half (about 10%, holding your memories) who lives in the outskirts and can reappear as a witness, an enemy, or an ally. That gives you the "unethical to create a half with no chance" cost as consequences, and the leader becomes the max-level example of the path you're tempted by. You keep a small point nerf if you want one, but the story does the moral work.
- **Derive the route from history, not a perk screen.** If unevenness is trained by doing it, track how uneven your past splits were, and let that set your cap and your bonuses. Your choices become the build, which is the Disco Elysium lock-in you liked.
- **Cap the number of bodies.** Total power grows at 1.2^n per split, so unbounded splitting produces armies. Your lore already has a brake: splitting needs a lot of food and mass beforehand, so use that as the hard gate and the points as the soft one.

Will your skill checks be threshold pass/fail like Disco Elysium, or something smoother? That decides how much depth beats breadth.
