Every bubble is separated by thin liquid walls, and where three walls meet they form liquid tubes called **Plateau borders**. - **gas in the bubbles and channels:** oxygen in through the pores, exhaust out the mouth
- **liquid in the Plateau borders:** the plasma-equivalent, moved by the same pressure pulses, or by slime-mold-style back-and-forth streaming

The saturated air keeps the borders from drying out. Nothing is a vessel, so "no veins" survives. And the liquid is spread thin through the whole body rather than collected in large vessels, so G-force resistance mostly survives too.

C also explains a couple of other things:
- **Bleeding:** a wound drains the borders, and liquid weeps out of the foam.
- **Collapse is lethal:** packed foam closes the gas channels, but liquid also floods the borders and the network stops working.

That gives a full cycle: the foam squeezes air along, each exhale pulls it toward the mouth, and the moving air draws fresh air in through the pores. Holding the valve slows all three steps, not just the venting.

**There's a real model for this: sea sponges.** Their phylum, Porifera ("pore-bearers"), has almost the same layout:
- **Many small intake pores** (ostia) all over the body.
- **A porous body** that water seeps through.
- **One large exhaust** (the osculum).
- **Flow past the exhaust** drags fluid out, which pulls fresh water in through the pores at no cost. That's Bernoulli's principle, the same effect that ventilates prairie-dog burrows.

Your pores work like that from the inside: air rushing past each pore's inner opening pulls outside air in. So intake is powered by circulation, not by the pore itself.

**Push and pull:**
- **Push:** the foam muscle squeezes air through the sponge.
- **Pull:** venting drops pressure at the mouth end, which steepens the gradient across the whole body.
- **Intake:** faster flow means stronger suction at every pore.
Each step speeds up the next, so a good giggle refreshes the whole body.

**Holding the valve fails in two ways:**
- **Toxin buildup:** waste can't leave.
- **Stale air:** circulation slows, so pore suction weakens and fresh air comes in slower too.
- **Inflation:** the body swells from intake that can't escape.

The **involuntary giggle** is the reflex that breaks the hold, the way the urge to breathe forces a held breath to end. That fits your earlier lines ([^mouth-close](wiki/biology/organs.md), [^feign-joy](wiki/biology/organs.md)): faking joy by holding pressure works briefly, then the body forces a vent.

It also makes talking and laughing physically good for you. Each one is an exhale that drives circulation, which links straight into the joy loop.

**One mechanical question:** if intake keeps going while the valve is held, inside pressure climbs above outside pressure. At some point the pores would leak *outward* unless something stops them. I see two options:
1. **One-way flaps (check valves).** Pores physically can't push air out, so inflation keeps building until the giggle reflex wins. Holding your breath gets dangerous over time.
2. **Self-limiting.** As circulation slows, pore suction weakens, so intake stalls once inside pressure matches it. Holding your breath is uncomfortable and toxic but plateaus, and you can't over-inflate by accident.

Which do you want? Option 1 makes deliberate inflation (combat, intimidation) stronger. Option 2 is safer and makes extra inflation a learned skill.
