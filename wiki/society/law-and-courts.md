## Overview

Giglian law [presumes an unequal split was intentional](#^presumption), rebuttable only by rarely provable duress, and [courts trust testimony more than humans do](#^testimony). Greedy mitosis is a [hot political debate](#^debate).

## Canon

- Giglian law presumes an unequal split was intentional, rebuttable only by rarely provable duress, and its courts lean heavily on testimony. [Z] (A, C)

### Greedy mitosis law

- Greedy (unequal) mitosis is a hot political debate, shaped like the pro-life debate but framed as "it's unethical to create a half that has no chance at success". [Z] (A) ^debate
- Mitosing is a conscious decision, so an unequal split is presumed intentional unless the Giglian proves it was induced under stress or duress. [Z] (A) ^presumption
- Proving coercion is about as possible as proving a child was conceived by rape: rare, but sometimes possible through circumstance and corroboration. [Z] (A)
- A case pits the greedy half against the discarded half. [Z] (A)
- If coercion is proven, neither half is culpable; liability falls on the coercer (another individual, an employer, or the state). [Z] (A)

### Testimony and history

- Courts generally trust testimony more than humans do. A culprit who has mitosed into 8 by trial means 8 witnesses. [Z] (C) ^testimony
- Courts were surely corrupt earlier in Giglian history, like pre-19th-century Earth, and this is taught in history class as the rise and fall of nations. [Z] (C)

## Unconfirmed

- Legal framing: wrongful-life claims. [C] (A)
- Mens rea with a duress excuse (strict liability with an excuse clause). [C] (A)
- The two halves share all pre-split memory, so trials are about interpretation and motivated reasoning, not hidden facts. [C] (A)
- Siblings who split after an event count as one source. [C] (C)
- Corrupt courts counted them as many (show trials). [C] (C)
- Disagreement between siblings is the forensic signal. [C] (C)
- "independent witness" defined by lineage distance. [C] (C)
- A split registry (timestamp and lineage of every mitosis) as the post-corruption reform and the first great bureaucracy. [C] (C)
- Whoever edits the split registry can hide purges. [C] (C)
- Untrained workers cannot aim a split, so presuming intent punishes them for bad luck; a reform movement could argue this. [C] (C)
- Intoxicated mitosis, chemical coercion to force an uneven split, and drug traces in a cast-off half as evidence of duress. [C] (C)
- Killings valued by how much unshared experience died. [C] (C)
- Liability attached to the whole branch to stop culprits splitting to dilute punishment. [C] (C)
- Forensic goop reading as a contested, state-controlled discipline. [C] (D)
