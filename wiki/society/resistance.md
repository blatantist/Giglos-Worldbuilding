## Overview

There are [two broad ways to topple the regime](#^two-routes): [revolution](#^sequence), built outward-in to seize the supply lines, or the much harder [peaceful council route](#^council). The inner ring [needs a stealth approach](#^stealth), and [terrorism labeling is emergent](#^emergent), not a hard rule. [Infiltrating the center is required on every path](#^infiltration).

## Canon

- There are two broad ways to topple the regime: revolution (outer ring plus middle ring seize the supply lines) or the much harder peaceful route of persuading enough of the inner government to veto or impeach the Founder. [Z] (C, F) ^two-routes

### Revolution

- The natural weakness is the over-developed center, which cannot feed itself. [Z] (C)
- Not a flat farming boycott: farmers could still feed themselves, but it would starve the middle rings. Instead the resistance must organize and convince the middle rings to join, then together commandeer shipments and serve only themselves. [Z] (C)
- This is treacherous because many are ingrained in the propaganda of the mythical, godlike Founder and see the resistance as terrorism. [Z] (C)
- Sequence: build a movement out of the outer ring, convince the middle ring, add their support and control of the supply lines, then bring the inner ring to its knees. Considerably easier than the peaceful route. [Z] (F) ^sequence
- Unrest or infighting caused by the protagonist can expose that the ruler has no skill left, letting the inner circle brand the Founder a fraud or puppet. [Z] (C)

### Peaceful route

- Social engineering through a council vote, not a democratic election. [Z] (F) ^council
- Ousting the top requires replacing enough of the government to veto or impeach. [Z] (F)
- The inner ring needs a "stealth" approach to be convinced peacefully. Failed persuasion makes inner-ring agents angry (or disgusted) and they label the persuader a terrorist. [Z] (G) ^stealth

### Rhetoric and labeling

- Revolutionary rhetoric spreads naturally inward until the upper class catches on and scorns it as terrorism. [Z] (F)
- The terrorism labeling is emergent from agents, not a hard rule, so the peaceful path stays possible. [Z] (F) ^emergent

### Infiltration

- Infiltrating the center is required on every path. The recruitment path likely has more middle-ring support (from convincing them to join), which gives easier access. [Z] (E) ^infiltration

### Middle-ring recruits

- The middle ring splits into aspirants (high stake, loyal) and the unaspiring (swing group); disillusioned aspirants are the best recruits. [C+] (G) ^aspirants

## Unconfirmed

- Ledgers of real yields vs quotas, held by middle-ring clerks, as the most persuasive evidence. [C] (C)
- The monks' archive as counter-history. [C] (C)
- Operational security by split timing: cells split before receiving sensitive details, since siblings share memory. [C] (C)
- The state's best counter is a staged blight timed to the first seized shipment. [C] (C)
- Flip the Randian frame: the outskirts farmers are the real producers and the core the looters. [C] (C)
- Supply squeezes can open persuasion options, so the two routes can be mixed. [C] (F)
- Violence raises threat labels that close inner-ring doors. [C] (F)
- Real-world framing: Gene Sharp's pillars of support, Chenoweth and Stephan on nonviolent campaigns, Granovetter threshold models, Snow and Benford framing theory. [C] (F)
