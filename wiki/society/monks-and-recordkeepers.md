## Overview

[Monks live for centuries in solitude](#^monks), holding historical knowledge, and the player [meets one who remembers the time before the city](#^game-monk). [Recordkeepers also live longer than most](#^recordkeepers). Chat L adds tentative ([Z?]) ideas: monks may be the only ones who remember the city's settlement, and they practice a shape-holding dance martial art a player could learn.

## Canon

### Monks

- Monks live simple lives for centuries in solitude, holding historical knowledge. Several live in each cloister, but they get no visitors. [Z] (C) ^monks
- In the game, the player meets a solitary recordkeeper monk who remembers the time before the city. [Z] (C) ^game-monk

### Recordkeepers

- Civilizations also have recordkeepers who live longer than most. [Z] (C) ^recordkeepers
- There is likely a cultural norm for what year or age you should mitose, like when to have a baby. [Z] (C)

## Tentative

- Perhaps only the monks could remember that the city-state settled or colonized the land: see [government ^settler](government-and-founder.md#^settler). [Z?] (L)
- Monks hone their growing ability and practice a dance martial art where they make and hold impressive shapes and change quickly and deftly. [Z?] (L) ^martial-art
- If a player studied under them, it would be invaluable for mobility and defensive/offensive capability, which makes the monks fun for the game. [Z?] (L)

## Unconfirmed

- Stillness keeps metabolism low so a cloister feeds many. [C] (C)
- No visitors protects the archive from spores and state-edited memories. [C] (C)
- Monks drift from current culture. [C] (C)
- Several monks may be halves of one person. [C] (C)
- Monks share memory by ritual fusion. [C] (C)
- The order may refuse to take sides. [C] (C)
- Recordkeepers split on a fixed schedule with halves posted to different offices. [C] (C)
- The longest-lived clerk is a prime bribery or assassination target. [C] (C)
- "Split too late" hoards unshared experience, "split too early" leaves both halves short. [C] (C)

### From chat L (Claude)

- Monks speak the conservative form (full-mode speech, older specifiers, original place names), so a monk's speech can itself prove there was a before. [C] (L)
- The art trains holding (sustaining extreme shapes, pressure endurance, the defensive layer), transitions (fast clean shape changes) and substrate memory stored in the tissue. [C] (L)
- Because a split halves the trained substrate, monks would avoid splitting for centuries or do clean splits. [C] (L)
- A monk's teaching could unlock mobility and combat skill-tree branches no one else offers; since they take no visitors, reaching one is a quest gate. [C] (L)
- Forms named with the broad-root-plus-specifier grammar; a depressed or strained student physically cannot hold the forms. [C] (L)
- The regime tolerates monks as harmless archivists; a combat tradition plus founder-era memory would make them a threat or a resource. [C] (L)
- A skilled monk who can seal pores selectively and hold form is hard to kill with gas, smoke or drowning. [C] (L)
