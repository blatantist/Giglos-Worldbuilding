## Overview

Zach wants [a full conlang derived from anatomy, grown alongside history and narrative](#^goal); it is [a worldbuilding tool, while the game ships in English](#^english). As a teaser of influences (J), the language [shares some resemblance to glaggle-speak](#^glaggle-speak); sound details are on [Phonology](phonology.md). Dutch is a possible influence on the vibe (J), tentative ([Z?]).

Chat L set the grammar's direction. The language should be [fun to speak and learn](#^fun) without [irregular conjugations and fiddly tense rules](#^no-annoyance). Building on Claude's proposals ([C+]), [provenance markers go first](#^markers-first) and [the future is a mood rather than a tense](#^future-mood), which Zach reads as [literalizing depression](#^no-future). Zach added [grammatical modes set by joy or environment](#^modes), [free-floating markers](#^free-markers), [grammatical tone and prosody only](#^grammatical-tone), and a lexicon of [broad roots made definite by context or specifiers](#^broad-roots). The language is [a base family fitted to biology and psychology, with city layers on top](#^layers), and the [ring gradient of dialects](#^ring-dialects) matters more than mainland speech, especially in [how outer dialects talk about negative valence](#^neg-valence-strategies). Sarcasm markers are tentative ([Z?]).

## Canon

- Zach wants a full Giglian conlang, derived from their anatomy and grown alongside history and narrative Tolkien-style; it is a worldbuilding tool, while the game itself ships in English. [Z] (E, F) ^goal

### Scope and method

- Game input and output text is English (or the player's language, a later localization issue). Giglian is for Zach's worldbuilding: deriving names, places and culture. [Z] (F) ^english
- A full conlang, not just morphemes, so idioms, syntax quirks, vocabulary and verbiage are well defined. English game text translates it directly, with Giglian loanwords for Giglian-specific actions and concepts humans lack words for. [Z] (F)
- Building the conlang is a main source of fun for Zach. [Z] (F)
- Method: language, worldbuilding and narrative developed together and piggybacking, so culture shapes how language spreads and changes and vice versa; the full narrative is written so the world breathes rather than sitting as ideas. [Z] (F)
- A full conlang is necessary for a culture with almost nothing in common with humans besides social order, so the Giglians do not read as "space Germany". Pointy-eared-human elves could do without one. [Z] (F)

### Influences (teaser, J28)

- The language shares some resemblance to the glaggle-speak of the glaggle internet meme: things like "thorn glognut, glaggle, giggler, enphoso", and other kinds of sounds like "gleeberland, thonglornius". See [glaggle origin](../world/naming-and-terminology.md#^glaggle). [Z] (J) ^glaggle-speak

### Design goal (L0)

- The conlang should be "fun to speak and learn", not meaning the simplicity of toki pona, but something that sounds nice, and has fun rules that add on without annoyance. [Z] (L) ^fun
- The kind of annoyance to avoid: some verbs having unique conjugations and lots of rules in how to specifically say something in the right tense. [Z] (L) ^no-annoyance
- Time in the language starts from the Giglian idea of past, present, future being based on the tree of experience due to mitosis, and the separate philosophy on whether it is previous/next life or contiguous past and present, all on top of the narrow slice of the current split's past and present, and an ambiguously narrow future. [Z] (L)

### Provenance markers and the future mood (L1–L11)

- Provenance markers on the experience tree as the core grammatical category, in place of tense. [C+] (L) ^provenance
  - Claude proposed it (L1); Zach built on it in L8. The specific three-layer set is under Unconfirmed.
- Provenance markers go first (clause-initial), so a speaker cut off by joy giggles loses only trailing detail, never the stance. [C+] (L) ^markers-first
  - Claude proposed it (L7); Zach used it as a jumping-off point in L8 ("I take just putting provenance markers first and raise you").
- The future is an optional intention/expectation mood ("growing toward"), not a tense. [C+] (L) ^future-mood
  - Claude's "fun rule 2" (L1); Zach: "rule 2 is fun" (L2).
- The future mood literalizes depression: joy is a good prediction of the future, and lack of joy, in lieu of a descriptor for negative valence, is literally having no future. [Z] (L) ^no-future
  - Builds on [^future-mood](#^future-mood) [C+].
- Different grammatical modes depending on joy or environment, like the formal/informal splits in human languages, but for specificity/possible verbosity versus efficiency and truncation. [Z] (L) ^modes
- The key markers are free floating: free to be dropped, moved around, repeated, etc. [Z] (L) ^free-markers
- Giggle-like punctuation and/or markers. [C+] (L) ^giggle-markers
  - Claude proposed giggle-shaped markers with a distinct contour (L11); Zach: "Yes I like the idea of giggle-like punctuation and/or markers" (L12).

### Tone and prosody (L12–L14)

- The direction implies a tonal language: the controlled tone of a calm utterance can give more markers, while a half-controlled, giggle-filled utterance would have less readable tone but still be understandable by other phonetics. [Z] (L)
  - Narrowed in L14 to grammatical tone only (next line).
- Just grammatical tone and prosody: calmer speech has more distinct rhythm and pitch, and high-joy giggles are more slurred/erratic. [Z] (L) ^grammatical-tone
  - Follows Claude's L13 advice against lexical tone. Speech physiology: [Phonology](phonology.md#^shaped-giggle).

### Lexicon: broad roots and specifiers (L18, L24)

- Joy is a less specific term than the wide breadth of terms for positive human valence. [Z] (L)
- English has many words that mean nearly the same thing but define a different specific quality (sharp, jagged, pointy, serrated). To make the language fun, Zach uses terms like Joy that are broad and become definite with context or specifiers. [Z] (L) ^broad-roots
- Motivation: English learners often mess up which specific synonym to use, because the specific meaning may betray what they mean to convey to the listener. [Z] (L)
- There would be separate words/concepts for deflating and for expelling air in a giggle and/or utterance. Exhaling is a form of deflation, but separate from bodily deflation, which is thought of more like shrinking from atrophy, though caused by lack of joy. [Z] (L) ^deflate-vs-exhale
  - "Leak" as the process of becoming depressed: see [joy and mood](../society/joy-and-mood.md#^leak-process).

### Language family and ring dialects (L30–L32)

- Start with a basic language family that fits their biology and psychology, then add onto that with the city's doctrinal, bureaucratic, and cultural influences. [Z] (L) ^layers
- A mainland language, if one is needed, would be the base plus modifications and new ideas replacing the city's additions. [Z] (L)
- Loanwords are not much of a worry. [Z] (L)
- Outer-speak is not as important for flavor as the city's own gradient of dialects/accents in the ring structure. [Z] (L) ^ring-dialects
- An outer dialect would have more creative ways to speak about negative valence: some people might sarcastically use positive language, another might use the dialect's deflationary terms, another who is heavily indoctrinated or deluded might masochistically speak positively of their pain. [Z] (L) ^neg-valence-strategies

### From chat L: grammar (Claude)

- Three provenance layers in one suffix slot, no irregulars: unmarked = this branch's own experience (current split); trunk marker = pre-split memory; record marker = knowledge from outside your tree (recordkeepers, history, hearsay). [C] (L)
- The trunk marker never says whether that past was "me" or "my parent", so the grammar stays neutral on the death-and-birth debate and each camp reads it differently. [C] (L)
- Split depth as reduplication: repeat the trunk marker once per split back, capped around three or four copies, then a single "deep" form, mirroring memory dilution and giving recordkeepers a prestige register. [C] (L)
- Negation as deflation: a deflation derivation applied to any word gives its absence or decay. [C] (L)
  - Claude later (L19) said it should rest on the atrophy sense, not the exhale sense, so exhaling stays neutral.
- No grammatical way to say "something bad is coming"; dread is phrased as the future mood being deflated or cut off. [C] (L)
- Threats become removals of future, which translate into English as oddly polite. [C] (L)
- Suicide as a double deflation, since it also ends descendants' marked futures. [C] (L)
- The future mood realized as something expensive (a rising pulse or energetic final burst), always available grammatically but convincing only with the valence to back it. [C] (L)
- The language needs to survive truncation by joy: head-first word order, with the most important content early. [C] (L)
- Repair conventions: a restart particle after a giggle break, listeners finishing the clause, deliberately truncated sentences as a stylistic device. [C] (L)
- Humor as a control contest: a joke works when it pushes the listener past their filter; comedians deliver high-joy content with steady articulation; courts reward flat control. [C] (L)
- Two pressures, two fixes: interruption by front-loading, acoustic or social noise by redundancy. Calm rooms get full mode, joyous or rushed speakers compressed mode, noisy-but-controlled speakers an emphatic repetitive mode. [C] (L)
- Shared tree as a compression license: siblings from one split speak near-telegraphically; strangers use full mode; courts require full mode for testimony; intimacy measured by how compressed you may be; compressing with a non-relative is a tell in infiltration scenes. [C] (L)
- Elites speak full mode everywhere as a display of control; workers default to compressed mode, which gets stigmatized as sloppy. [C] (L)
- Persistence: the last marker stays in force until replaced; unmarked means current branch only at the start of a conversation. [C] (L)
- Position carries scope: a marker before a clause covers it; after a word it is a late correction or hedge; repetition re-anchors after a gap or adds emphasis. [C] (L)
- Markers are giggle-shaped but with a distinct contour, so real uncontrolled giggles (flat or ragged contours) can be told from a stance; a misparsed stance becomes a comedic or dramatic event. [C] (L)
- Tone as a grammatical and pragmatic layer: a robust segmental channel carries words and marker identity; a fragile tonal channel adds refinements (confidence, surprise, split depth, stance vs hedge). [C] (L)
- Valence as a bandwidth knob: mild joy adds melodic flourish, strain flattens contours, overflowing joy randomizes pitch. [C] (L)
- Courts and recordkeepers demand full tonal resolution, so testimony is sung with precision; loud-floor workers speak segment-only. [C] (L)
- A beginner can ignore tone and still be understood, sounding flat and a bit childlike. [C] (L)
- Song and poetry as the prestige art (high joy with controlled rhythm); diplomacy and courts reward flat delivery; intimacy is being allowed to slur. [C] (L)

### From chat L: lexicon (Claude)

- Broad roots plus specifiers is a generic head with an optional specifier; specifiers drop first in strained, noisy or giggly speech. [C] (L)
- English's synonym pile mostly comes from layered borrowing (Germanic base, French and Latin on top); a city/outskirts split could produce such layers later. [C] (L)
- Recordkeepers and healers need frozen specific compounds, a register gap between everyday and specialist speech. [C] (L)
- One root family for vigor, joy, health and speaking ease, with derivations splitting them. [C] (L)
- No clean word for "emotional pain" versus "physical pain", only where and how much pressure is lost. [C] (L)
- Vocabulary for exhale/utterance, overflow burst, leak (loss of baseline), and deflation/atrophy (resulting state). [C] (L)
- Words for the nudge that tips you into a giggle (probably the root of "to speak"), riding the running giggle, and stifling. [C] (L)
- A "good leak versus bad leak" idiom, if joy-giggling counts as leaking. [C] (L)

### From chat L: family, dialects, history (Claude)

- The city's language as a daughter language in a Tolkien-style family; mainland cousins share the giggle-source speech and provenance grammar, diverging in vocabulary, registers and marker strictness. [C] (L)
- Redacted history leaves fossils: toponyms, loanwords and frozen compounds from the pre-settlement land survive a mitosis purge; possibly substrate words from an earlier group. [C] (L)
- The trunk marker becomes politically loaded: claiming shared trunk is a loyalty claim, disputing which trunk memories are real is heresy. [C] (L)
- One regular loanword rule, a native-vs-borrowed tag in the lexicon, and mainland grammar left open. [C] (L)
  - Zach replied that loanwords are not much of a worry (L30).
- Outskirts speech carries loanwords, looser marker discipline and contact-jargon habits, heard by the center as impure; a purist "founder speech" ideology. [C] (L)
- The city layer thins with distance from the center and the base layer shows through, giving a dialect continuum. [C] (L)
- Prestige pull inward: middle-ring hypercorrection (over-marking provenance, full mode in the wrong places). [C] (L)
- Work conditions (noise, long shifts, hunger) push outskirts speech toward compressed mode and strained prosody. [C] (L)
- Doctrinal vocabulary diffuses outward with a lag and drifts in meaning, ending loose or ironic: center words turned sarcastic as a resistance vocabulary. [C] (L)
- The outskirts keep older base forms, so outer-ring speech is "impure" yet closer to the base language. [C] (L)
- Passing as center-born is a player skill heard through prosody control, marker discipline and doctrinal word use. [C] (L)
- Tag each word with its layer (base, bureaucratic, doctrinal, cultural). [C] (L)

### From chat L: talking about negative valence (Claude)

- Sarcasm is positive words with strained or flat prosody; a skilled sarcast fakes strain while staying articulate. [C] (L)
- Each dialect lexicalizes the atrophy root differently, e.g. quantity gaps ("short of joy by a hand") or shrinking metaphors tied to local work. [C] (L)
- Masochistic positivity is given away by prosody (too controlled, or a forced source against real low pressure); it peaks in the middle ring and the barracks edges. [C] (L)
- Dissociation through provenance: pushing pain into the trunk or record markers to describe suffering without owning it. [C] (L)
- Understatement and measurement, like "I'm a little under". [C] (L)
- Three deliveries: prosody only (deniable, intimate), prosody plus marker (unmistakable), marker only (dry or bureaucratic irony). [C] (L)
- Deflationary markers bleach into intensifiers through overuse and get replaced, so each region's marker is at a different stage of that cycle. [C] (L)
- Prosody doesn't survive transcription but markers do: dissidents wanting deniability stay prosody-only, the regime needs trained listeners, and written Giglian looks more positive than spoken. [C] (L)
