## Overview

Stub (physiology only, no inventory yet). [Phonology comes from physiology](#^physiology): a [fused tongue that makes the mouth a variable-length tube](#^tube), [no nose](#^no-nose), and [speech that sounds like talking with the tongue held back](#^sound). The beak is tentative [Z?].

Chat J added a teaser, not an inventory: [G-type sounds are common](#^g-common), because Zach [speaks "like a giglian" with the tongue in the back of the throat](#^tongue-back), with [m, n, t, s adapted to the middle/front palate](#^adapted) and [T and S kept back from the palatal ridge](#^ts). The language [resembles glaggle-speak](conlang.md#^glaggle-speak). The posh-sounding center is tentative [Z?]. How m and n work without a nose was raised as [OPEN] (now resolved); Zach then said [sounds their biology doesn't support are romanized to the closest IPA sounds](#^romanized) for human readers, and [his held-nose tests](#^canada) show a short m and a passing n still work.

## Canon

- Phonology comes from physiology; the goal is something humans can still roughly pronounce. [Z] (E) ^physiology
- The human-tongue equivalent is completely fused to the floor of the mouth, so the mouth is a variable-length tube opened and closed by a fleshy tongue-like pad. [Z] (E) ^tube
- No nose. [Z] (E) ^no-nose
- Speech sounds like a human talking with the tongue held back in the throat; dental-fricative-like sounds are made with the middle of the tongue against the palate. [Z] (E) ^sound
- Zach tested this himself and can speak pretty well with those restrictions while holding nose and velum closed, so the inventory is richer than Claude first predicted. [Z] (E)
- Speech is controlled giggling, and "giggle" is only the English name for that vocalization type. [Z] (D, F)

### Sounds and accent (teaser, J28)

- G-type sounds are common to the language. [Z] (J) ^g-common
- The way Zach has been speaking like a giglian is with his tongue in the back of his throat, which is where /g/ /k/ is. [Z] (J) ^tongue-back
- m, n, t, s, etc. can be adapted to the more middle/front part of the palate. [Z] (J) ^adapted
  - Compare [no nose](#^no-nose) [Z] (E); see the m/n line under Unconfirmed and the m/n open question.
- Zach has been making T and S sounds at the palatal ridge, although he tries to keep the tongue farther back than that to simulate a more tubular mouth without teeth/gums to create such a ridge. [Z] (J) ^ts

### Nasals and romanization (J33)

- Nasals and similar sounds that aren't supported by their biology would be adapted, in the sense that they'd be romanized to the closest-sounding IPA sounds for the sake of being readable by a human. [Z] (J) ^romanized
- For m and n, Zach can still make those sounds while holding his nose and making sure not to inflate his held nose; he can say something like "canada". [Z] (J) ^canada
- He can't hold the n sound, but he can make the passing consonant. [Z] (J)
- A short hold for an M sound works if it inflates the cheeks. [Z] (J)
- A held N doesn't work, because that blocks off the mouth and only reverberates the nose. [Z] (J)

## Tentative

- No teeth. Probably a cartilaginous beak as part of the lips: mouth corners more flexible, center and behind stiffer so they can bite. Whether they need a hard surface depends on how they eat, still undecided. [Z?] (E)
- Giglians nearer to the center sound like very silly posh Englishmen. [Z?] (J) ^posh-center
- The weather actually kinda fits that (the posh-English sound of the center); see [Giglos as a world](../world/giglos-world.md). [Z?] (J)
- Presumably they'd not only lose some sounds but also gain some new ones, from being more like a pink trombone than the actual human vocal tract. [Z?] (J) ^gain-sounds

## Unconfirmed

- No nasal contrast. [C] (E)
- Most tongue-tip consonants collapse into pad-against-palate places plus labial, pharyngeal and glottal sounds. [C] (E)
- Vowel quality from tube shape and lip rounding. [C] (E)
- Contrast shifted to length, pitch and phonation. [C] (E)
- Variable tube length as a global formant knob for prosody. [C] (E)
- Giggle pulses as syllable nuclei. [C] (E)
- Beak taps as click-like consonants. [C] (E)
- Caveats: Zach still has a mobile tongue body, and perception (what listeners can distinguish) limits phonemes more than production; record and compare spectrograms. [C] (E)

### From J29 (Claude, on the J28 teaser)

- Back of the tongue (velar): g, k, and the "gh" in Dutch *goed* are the core Giglian sounds. [C] (J)
- Farther back (uvular): French "r", German "ach", Arabic "q"; fits "tongue in the back of the throat" even better. [C] (J)
- Middle of the tongue on the palate (palatal): German "ich", and a t/d said with the tongue middle; where Zach's T and S end up. [C] (J)
- T and S made with the tongue pulled back off the ridge come out as something between "ty" and "ch", and between "sh" and German "ich". [C] (J)
- That is a recognisable, consistent accent, not just a mushier English. [C] (J)
- m and n need a nose (air out through the nose); with no nose, Giglian "m" and "n" would come out as something else: a hum against closed lips that bursts out like a soft "b" or "d", or a sound with the cheeks puffing out. [C] (J)
- A "plummy" upper-class English accent comes from holding the tongue back and the throat open, so central Giglians sounding posh is the same physiology pushed further; their prestige accent would be the most "throat-back" one. [C] (J)
- The grey, damp, overcast weather adds to the British feel. [C] (J)
- An accent that changes from the city center outward could tie into the [center/entrance evaluation](../society/center-entrance-evaluation.md). [C] (J)

### From J34 (Claude, on Zach's m/n tests)

- A held "m" with puffed cheeks is a voiced "b" sound stretched out: the voice keeps going while the cheeks inflate to give the air somewhere to go, and stops when they're full. [C] (J)
- A Giglian could do this better, because their whole mouth-tube can change length and widen. [C] (J)
- So Giglian "m" is probably a long, soft, cheek-puffed "b" sound, written as "m". [C] (J)
- The short "n" in "canada": with the nose pinched, the nasal cavity is still there behind the fingers, so a little air and sound can go into it for a moment; that is the brief "n", and why it can't be held. [C] (J)
- Giglians have no nasal cavity at all (resting on [no nose](#^no-nose) [Z] (E)), so their "n" would be closer to a soft "d" or a "d" further back: the "talking with a cold" sound, "Cadada". [C] (J)
- Sounds gained (fitting the Pink Trombone comparison): long, cheek-puffed stops like Zach's "m" could be their own type of sound. [C] (J)
  - J34 also lists sliding tube length (the whole voice gliding deeper or higher mid-word), giggle pulses as syllable cores, and beak taps as clicks ("if the beak becomes canon"); these restate the (E) [C] lines above and are not duplicated.
