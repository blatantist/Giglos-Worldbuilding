## Overview

Giglians are [distributed, slime-mold-like organisms whose growth is consciously steered by bioelectric signals](#^core). The body is [frame plus foam](#^frame-foam), [growth control is a trained, inherited skill](#^growth-skill), and they [never die of old age](#^senescence). Circulation runs through the foam ([organs ^foam-circulation](organs.md#^foam-circulation)), which resolved tension T2; the base form (T7) is still a recorded tension. Giglians [grow if they eat enough](#^eat-grow) and [only balloon in mass when inactive](#^balloon); [body mass is their breath tank](organs.md#^tank-mass).

## Canon

- Giglians are distributed, slime-mold-like organisms whose cells share one genome and whose growth is consciously steered by bioelectric signals, inspired by Michael Levin's bioelectricity research. [Z] (A) ^core

### Body plan and tissue

- A mix between a giant unicellular organism and a mycelial network; "slime people" with a cartilage-like skeleton. [Z] (A)
- No concept of gender. [Z] (A)
- Hybrid body: cartilaginous structural ("frame") tissue plus foamy tissue that acts roughly as muscle. Giglians do not really have muscles. [Z] (D) ^frame-foam
- Cells have no normal cell wall. [Z] (D)
- Neural substrate is distributed in nerve-like cells throughout the body. [Z] (A)
- Skill ("muscle memory") lives in localized neural substrate in the tissue; a pianist's arm holds substrate trained for playing. [Z] (D)
- No veins or bloodflow, which makes them G-force resistant. [Z] (A)

### Growth and shape

- Cells with identical genome can be induced to grow different parts by electrical signal; Giglians control those signals consciously, fictionally sped up so they can grow arms at will. [Z] (A)
- Growth control is a trained skill, like fine motor skill. With no childhood, it is inherited from whichever ancestor in the split lineage learned it (walking, squishing through gaps, stretching an arm to a shelf). [Z] (A) ^growth-skill
- Different control specializations (growth, density or rheology shifting, etc.) are like human skills (singing, running, lifting) that each person takes interest in and practices. [Z] (A)
- Deformation conserves mass. A well-trained Giglian can aerate (whip cytoplasm into foam) to increase volume at constant mass; "aeration, not inflation". [Z] (A)
- Limb growth looks smooth and bulk-flowing, like Jake from Adventure Time but toned down, not spindly or webby. [Z] (C)
- The frame is grown like arms are: a hip-like or thorax-like area can be shaped by attention; whether a species can grow a "pelvis" at all is genetic. The genome is the plan, intentional changes are possible (the bird analog's hand-shaped wings). [Z] (D)
- Frog-eyes-on-butt precedent: a Giglian could grow an arm on its back. [Z] (D)
- Homeostatic growth runs subconsciously for the whole body; changing shape is intentional. [Z] (D)

### Low gravity (Chat M; Zach: [lower gravity would suit gelatinous beings](../world/giglos-world.md#^low-g))

- The pressure a body needs to hold its shape grows with gravity × height; at half Earth gravity a giglian of the same height needs about half the holding pressure to stand, leaving more of the budget for circulation, speech and joy. [C] (M)
- Under low gravity the square-cube squeeze sets in later, which helps a somewhat humanoid base form without a skeleton. [C] (M)
- Falls hurt less: a gel body hits the ground at lower speed; this changes combat and makes climbing easier. [C] (M)

### From chat N (Claude)

- The foam does three jobs: muscle (frame plus foam), the volume trick (aeration) and lung. [C] (N)
- "No veins" still holds, since the channels are foam, not vessels; G-force resistance survives if what moves is mostly gas, and weakens if a liquid carrier also circulates. [C] (N)
- Bleeding: a wound vents pressure and leaks cytoplasm, closer to a punctured tire than a cut artery; with the Plateau-border network, a wound drains the borders and liquid weeps out of the foam. [C] (N)
